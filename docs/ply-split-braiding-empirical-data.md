# Ply-Split Braiding: Empirical Data

> Measured observations from real ply-split work, kept separate from the structural
> theory in [Ply-Split Braiding: Foundations and Existing Resources](./ply-split-braiding-foundations.md).
> These are working numbers for estimating and sanity-checking designs (e.g. finished
> dimensions), not derived from first principles — treat each entry as a rule of thumb
> until it's been checked against more samples, and record the conditions it was
> observed under whenever they're known.

## Dimensions

| Observation | Value | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished width vs. combined cord width | Finished width ≈ **60%** of the combined width of all cords used (cords laid side by side, unsplit) | Cords interlock and pull in during splitting, so the finished braid is narrower than the sum of its parts. Material, cord diameter, ply count, and splitting density likely all affect the exact ratio — this figure hasn't yet been broken down by those variables. |

## Open questions

- Does the 60% ratio hold across cord counts, or does it drift with wider/narrower
  pieces?
- How much does cord material (goat hair vs. cotton) or ply count change the ratio?
- Is there an equivalent shrinkage ratio for length (working length vs. finished
  length)?
